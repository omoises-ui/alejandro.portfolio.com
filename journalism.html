Journalism
as an A&R
Advantage

Before creative direction and artist development, I trained and worked as a journalist and broadcast producer in Venezuela. That background sharpened the skills I now consider central to A&R: research, interviewing, pattern recognition, editorial judgement, audience awareness and the ability to identify the story that makes a person or project worth paying attention to.

Editorial development

Journalism taught me to ask better questions before forming an opinion. In A&R, that matters: Who is this artist? What is genuinely distinctive here? What is signal and what is noise? Why could this connect now?

Broadcast production also trained me to work under pressure, structure information quickly and collaborate across editorial and production teams — experience that translates directly into artist development environments where creative and business decisions need to move together.

Television Reporting

At just 21, I was already working both in front of the camera and behind it, helping bring television reports and special programs on music and extreme sports to life. It was a formative experience that gave me a solid understanding of how media works — from research and production to editorial framing and on-camera communication. Today, that knowledge directly strengthens my A&R perspective: I understand not only how to identify and develop an artist, but also how to shape a story and proposition that media outlets can recognise, program and bring to wider audiences.

Broadcast production · Venezuela

Behind the Broadcast

Production experience around established Venezuelan media figures strengthened my understanding of preparation, editorial structure, pacing, talent and audience attention.

01

César Miguel Rondón

I worked in production around programs hosted by César Miguel Rondón, one of the most influential voices in Venezuelan and Latin American radio and journalism. A journalist, broadcaster and author with a career spanning decades, he is also widely known for El Libro de la Salsa, a landmark work on Caribbean popular music. Working around this level of broadcast sharpened my sense of editorial preparation, topic selection, timing and the production systems that support strong on-air talent.

02

Oscar Yanes

I worked in television production around Oscar Yanes, a pioneer of Venezuelan audiovisual journalism and a three-time winner of Venezuela's National Journalism Prize. His career ranged from war correspondence in Vietnam to some of the country's most recognisable interview and current-affairs programs. Working around his television projects reinforced how personality, storytelling and editorial framing can turn information into memorable media.
