Journalism
as an A&R
Advantage

Before creative direction and artist development, I trained and worked as a journalist and broadcast producer in Venezuela. That background sharpened the skills I now consider central to A&R: research, interviewing, pattern recognition, editorial judgement, audience awareness and the ability to identify the story that makes a person or project worth paying attention to.

Editorial development

Journalism taught me to ask better questions before forming an opinion. In A&R, that matters: Who is this artist? What is genuinely distinctive here? What is signal and what is noise? Why could this connect now?

Broadcast production also trained me to work under pressure, structure information quickly and collaborate across editorial and production teams — experience that translates directly into artist development environments where creative and business decisions need to move together.

Selected archive · National television

Television Reporting

At just 21, as a newly graduated journalist, I was already working both in front of the camera and behind it on some of the country’s most visible and relevant television productions, across major broadcasters such as Venevisión and Televen. For me, it was a dream fulfilled: moving directly from journalism school into national television and helping bring reports and special programs on music, culture and extreme sports to life. That experience gave me a solid understanding of how media works — from research and production to editorial framing, live coordination and on-camera communication. Today, that knowledge directly strengthens my A&R perspective: I understand not only how to identify and develop an artist, but also how to shape a story and proposition that media outlets can recognise, program and bring to wider audiences.

01

César Miguel Rondón

César Miguel Rondón · Journalist, writer, broadcaster and author of El Libro de la Salsa

César Miguel Rondón is one of Venezuela’s most respected journalists and writers and a major reference in Latin American media. His professional career began in 1974 and has spanned more than five decades across radio, television, press, writing, production and cultural journalism. He is also the author of El Libro de la Salsa, a landmark chronicle of Caribbean urban music.

I worked for César Miguel Rondón at 6J86 Producciones, his production company, under executive producer Laura Rodríguez. Rondón is one of the most influential voices in Venezuelan and Latin American journalism, radio and cultural broadcasting, and the author of El Libro de la Salsa, a landmark work on Caribbean urban music. Working inside that production environment sharpened my standards for research, editorial preparation, scripting, timing and the production systems required to support high-profile talent.

02

Oscar Yanes

Óscar Yanes · Venezuelan journalist, writer and chronicler

I worked in television production around Oscar Yanes, a legend of Venezuelan journalism and one of the country's great television storytellers. A pioneer of audiovisual journalism and a three-time winner of Venezuela's National Journalism Prize, Yanes turned the country's history, characters and collective memory into compelling television. The programs I produced in this period were especially connected to Venezuelan history — researching stories, verifying context, structuring material and helping transform historical information into accessible television.
