Case 02

Best Destinations

Big Data · Creative Concept · Guerrilla Marketing · Social Impact

Developed at Rapp Collins Worldwide for the Argentine market, this campaign for UNICEF Argentina set out to improve the effectiveness of its digital content in attracting new donors.

The challenge was significant: the available media budget was extremely limited, restricting the campaign mainly to simple web banners and Facebook click-to-web formats. At first, the project seemed to offer very little room for creativity.

Through Big Data analysis, we identified and combined two behavioral statistics that revealed an unexpected opportunity. That intersection allowed us to leverage Argentina’s spectacular natural beauty as a hook and transform a media limitation into the starting point of a high-impact guerrilla marketing campaign developed with a budget close to zero.

The Insight

The starting point came from the intersection of two behavioral statistics. We discovered that 7 out of 10 Argentinians click on travel-related content, while only 3 out of 10 click on donation-related content.

That contrast revealed an opportunity: instead of asking people to pay attention directly to a fundraising message, we could first attract them through something they were already naturally interested in — travel and Argentina’s spectacular landscapes.

The gap between what people wanted to click on and what UNICEF needed them to engage with became the strategic foundation of the idea.

The Idea

That intersection between data, behavior and cultural interest became the foundation of Best Destinations.

We used Argentina’s natural beauty and the visual language associated with travel content as the campaign’s attention mechanism. Instead of competing directly for attention with a conventional donation message, the campaign leveraged a type of content that already generated spontaneous interest among users.

In this way, we transformed a highly constrained digital brief into a guerrilla marketing idea capable of generating impact by using audience behavior itself as a media advantage.

My Role

I worked on the project as Creative Copywriter & Creative Project Manager.

My role combined creative concept development with project execution. I contributed to shaping the strategic connection between the data, audience behavior and the creative idea, while also developing the narrative and copy and coordinating the creative process through implementation.

How I Think

Using data not just to understand behavior, but to discover an unexpected creative opportunity — and turning a media limitation into the core of the idea.
