The Insight

Cyberbullying was often dismissed by adults as “kids being kids on the internet.”

But unlike traditional bullying, digital aggression can be persistent, large-scale and constant.

It does not necessarily stop when a child leaves school. It can follow them into their bedroom, their family life and the moments when they are supposed to feel safe.

The Idea

We made digital aggression physical.

Facebook notifications, Instagram interactions and video-game messages were represented as actual blows coming from an invisible attacker.

The campaign placed these attacks inside ordinary situations: in a bedroom, during a family outing or while returning home after football.

By translating an invisible online experience into something physically recognizable, the campaign aimed to make the seriousness of cyberbullying impossible to dismiss.

My Role

I worked as Creative Director & Copywriter, contributing to the concept, narrative and development of both the audiovisual and graphic campaign.

How I Think

When a problem is difficult to see, make people feel its consequences in a language they cannot ignore.
