Azul Animal

01Sentidos
0:003:45

Azul Animal was my first experience building a music project from the ground up. As vocalist, songwriter and creative lead, I contributed not only to the songs, but also to the development of the band’s identity, digital presence and live proposition.

At a time when MySpace had become an essential platform for independent artists, we built a community of more than 13,000 followers, primarily across Venezuela and Mexico. That international connection led to Sentidos and Agorafobia being included in Alter-Nation: Compilado Alternativo Mexicano, released by Carpe Diem Records México.

The project’s visual identity was built around a distinctive use of colour and carried consistently across social media, promotional material and our multimedia EP. In parallel, I worked on strengthening the live show, overseeing the technical rider as well as the artistic and operational details of each performance.

Azul Animal received airplay on programmes dedicated to alternative music in Caracas and other Venezuelan cities, and performed in emblematic spaces within the independent circuit, including the Moulin Rouge Band Festival.

Azul Animal became my first school in artist development. It taught me that a band is built not only through its songs, but through the integration of music, identity, production, communication and audience connection.

This compilation opened the door to the underground market in Mexico City and its surrounding scene, helping us grow to more than 13,000 MySpace followers.

Todos los Monstruos

01Flashback
0:003:19

I founded Todos los Monstruos in Caracas in 2009 and led its development as both a musical project and a multidisciplinary creative universe. By 2012, the sound we had developed in the studio—blending genres, electronic rhythms and technology—had become genuinely distinctive within the scene and its moment.

In collaboration with Venezuelan visual artist Jean Piero Montilla, I created a distinctive conceptual language connecting the music with artwork, flyers, press materials, storytelling and live performance. The project combined songwriting and electronic experimentation with Ableton Live and unconventional controllers—including PlayStation controllers used for real-time sampling.

Press & cultural impact

From an underground project to national coverage.

As a result of the work across music production, live production and press strategy, we achieved a level of media coverage that was significant for an underground, fully independent artist.

Selected interviews

Conversations in the media

The video below brings together a selection of this coverage and documents the live-event production developed through the Todos los Monstruos collective. For our most important shows, we expanded the concert format into a multidisciplinary showcase featuring artists from different disciplines.

Our live performances also became a space for continued experimentation. They were ultimately one of the main reasons the band gained recognition in Caracas and across the country.

Todos los Monstruos was selected for Festival Nuevas Bandas, an important Latin American platform for emerging music. It achieved national radio rotation, extensive press coverage and appearances at major festivals, including Caracas Rock before more than 13,000 people. The release artwork was submitted for Latin Grammy consideration.

Beyond the music, I assembled and led a team including management, road management and press. I also created the Todos los Monstruos collective, expanding concerts into multidisciplinary events with circus performers, visual artists and guest musicians.

A defining lesson

At the height of the project, I signed an agreement with a Colombian record label that prevented me from releasing new music for more than five years. It became a defining lesson in artist rights, contracts and long-term release strategy. During that period, I began developing #HarakiriRobot—an alternative creative path that would fully re-emerge years later in Berlin.

#Harakiri
Robot

Buenos Aires · Mexico City · Berlin · 2014—Present
Synopsis / Love ist verboten

“We have everything, but we have nothing.”

In a world that has achieved everything it once desired—beauty, order, comfort and control—people discover what they gave up to get there: the ability to love. The more perfect life becomes, the more it loses what matters most. Against this backdrop, Amendment 999 grants new rights to automata that begin to show human qualities. Their presence raises the story’s central question: what is left of us when we have everything except love?

Built from this conceptual premise, the project extends beyond music into an AI film created with AI actors and a book. We are currently beginning a crowdfunding process to take the already pre-produced songs into the studio and produce them professionally.

Release Strategy

#HarakiriRobot began during the contractual period that prevented me from releasing new music with Todos los Monstruos. Initially developed between Buenos Aires and Mexico City, it remained largely unreleased before I resumed and reimagined it in Berlin in 2024.

The heart of this cyberpunk, post-apocalyptic story is love: in the pursuit of a perfect life, we gain everything and lose what matters most. The planned record, AI-produced film and book explore that premise through different perspectives.
